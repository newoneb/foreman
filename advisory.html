Sale readiness

A confidential review that shows an owner the company as a buyer and a lender will see it, while there is still time to act.

The review covers

  • Normalized earnings. Reported results restated for one-time items, owner compensation and related-party rent.
  • Add-back review. Each proposed adjustment tested for documentation and recurrence.
  • Indicative value range. Based on comparable transactions in the sector.
  • Lender coverage test. Whether a buyer could finance the price under current SBA standards.
  • Owner dependence. Customers, relationships, licenses and knowledge held by the owner alone.
  • Property options. Where the owner holds the real estate, the effect of each choice on the transaction.
  • Priority actions. The changes that would most improve value or financeability, in order.
  • Readout. A written report and a working session with the owner and, if invited, their advisors.
02

Valuation and financing

What a company can sell for depends on what a buyer can finance. We analyze both together.

Comparable transactions

Multiples from closed sales of similar companies, adjusted for size, growth and the quality of earnings.

Lender standards

Most SBA-financed acquisitions now require 1.25x debt service coverage on historical cash flow and an independent valuation.

Transaction structure

How the buyer’s equity, seller financing and the treatment of the property change the price a buyer can pay.

We test an owner’s expected price against what a typical buyer could borrow, so that expectations and financing agree before a sale begins. Our ranges are estimates for planning. A lender financing the purchase will rely on its own independent valuation.

03

Succession planning

A sale is one path among several. We compare the options an owner actually has.

Transfer to family
Continuity and control over timing. The questions are the next generation’s readiness, fairness among heirs and how the owner is paid.
Sale to management or employees
Buyers who already know the business. These sales are often financed partly by the owner over time, which leaves some of the owner’s value at risk.
Sale to an outside buyer
Individual buyers, search funds, competitors and private equity. Usually the most cash at closing, with the most diligence.

Legal structure and tax treatment fall outside our scope. We work alongside the owner’s attorney and CPA on both.

04

Operating performance

The changes that raise a company’s value are usually operational, and they take time to show up in the numbers.

  • Pricing and margin

    Where prices lag costs, and which customers or services earn the least.

  • Customer concentration

    How much revenue depends on a few accounts, and how to reduce that exposure.

  • Working capital

    Receivables, inventory and payables, and the cash tied up in each.

  • Owner dependence

    Delegation, documentation and a second line of management.

  • Financial reporting

    Monthly closes and statements that a lender can rely on.

  • Recurring revenue

    Service agreements and contracts that make earnings more predictable.

Engagement

How an engagement works

Most reviews take a few weeks from the day we receive the company’s records.

  1. Introduction

    A conversation about the company and the owner’s questions. We sign a non-disclosure agreement before any figures are shared.

  2. Records

    Three years of tax returns or financial statements, a current profit and loss statement, payroll and property details.

  3. Analysis and review

    A student team prepares the analysis. A second team reviews every figure before delivery.

  4. Readout

    We present the findings in person or by video. The report belongs to the owner, who decides who else sees it.

Scope

What we don’t do

We work alongside the professionals an owner already trusts, and send any work that requires a license back to them.

  • Broker transactions or introduce buyers

  • Provide appraisals or fairness opinions

  • Give tax, legal or investment advice

  • Charge fees, commissions or referral payments

  • Contact employees, customers or lenders without the owner’s permission

Questions

Common questions

Why is the work provided without charge?

Foreman is a student organization. Our members learn by doing real work to a professional standard, and owners receive analysis they would otherwise pay for. We do not charge fees, take commissions or accept referral payments.

Who sees a company’s figures?

Only the members working on the engagement and the team that reviews their work, all bound by the non-disclosure agreement. We share the report with an owner’s advisors only at the owner’s request. When the engagement ends, we return or delete the records.

Is the value range an appraisal?

No. It is an estimate for planning. When a buyer uses an SBA loan, the lender must obtain an independent valuation from a qualified source, and the financing rests on that figure. Our review shows an owner in advance what that valuation is likely to examine.

Does an owner need to be planning a sale?

No. Many owners ask for a review three to five years ahead, while there is time to act on the priority actions. Others want to compare a sale with bringing in a manager or transferring the company to family.

Which companies do you work with?

Owner-operated companies in Chicago and the surrounding suburbs, mostly in commercial and home services, distribution, light industrial and business services, with at least two years of tax returns or financial statements.
