Real Estate

For many owners, the building is the other half of the decision.

Owner-operators often hold their property in a separate company and lease it to the business. At the point of succession or sale, what happens to the building changes the price, the financing and the owner’s income afterward.

Discuss a property

Owner-occupied property

Two companies, one owner

The rent the business pays the property company is often set informally, years before anyone thinks about a sale. A buyer will reset it to market.

−$48,000

Reduction in adjusted EBITDA when related-party rent is set to market in our illustrative review. The value does not disappear. At the midpoint of the illustrative range, about $160,000 of enterprise value moves from the company to the property, which is why the two decisions belong together.

Three paths

Sell it, lease it, or sell it and lease it back

We compare each path using the owner’s actual figures, alongside the analysis of the company itself.

Sell with the company

Owner of the building after
The buyer
How it works
The buyer acquires the company and the property in one transaction.
The owner receives
The most cash at closing.
Considerations
A larger purchase to finance. SBA 7(a) loans can carry real estate for up to 25 years, against 10 for equipment and working capital.

Retain and lease to the buyer

Owner of the building after
The current owner
How it works
The owner sells the company and signs a lease with its new owner.
The owner receives
Rent over the lease term, and continued ownership of the property.
Considerations
Rent must be at market. The buyer’s lender will look closely at the length of the lease and its renewal options.

Sale-leaseback

Owner of the building after
An investor
How it works
The property is sold to an investor, and the company signs a long-term lease.
The owner receives
Cash for the property, separate from the sale of the company.
Considerations
The company takes on market rent. Investors price on the rent, the length of the lease and the strength of the tenant.

SBA loan terms from the City of Chicago’s SBA 7(a) program summary.

The analysis

What we examine

  • Market rent

    What comparable space leases for, and how the current related-party rent compares.

  • Lease terms

    Length, renewal options, escalations, and who pays taxes, insurance and maintenance.

  • Financing

    How each path changes what a buyer must borrow, and on what terms.

  • Valuation context

    How investors and appraisers are likely to view the property as a leased asset.

  • Timing

    Whether to settle the property question before, during or after the sale of the company.

  • Tax coordination

    Each path carries different tax consequences for the owner’s CPA to review. We prepare the figures they will need.

Foreman does not appraise property, broker real estate or give tax advice. A licensed appraiser, a real estate broker and the owner’s CPA handle those.
